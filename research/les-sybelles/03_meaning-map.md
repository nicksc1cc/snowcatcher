# Les Sybelles — meaning map and narrative architecture

**Status:** Working editorial map, 21 October 2026

## Central proposition

Les Sybelles' appeal is the size and variety of a linked ski area spread across the Maurienne's high ground, but its headline scale must be read through the shape of the actual network. The five core resorts share connected skiing around the Ouillon; Saint-Colomban's former link is a material exception, not a detail to hide inside the total.

## What the reader should understand

- What “Les Sybelles” refers to and how the operator defines the ski area.
- Which parts form a continuous lift-and-piste network.
- Why the Ouillon high ground matters to the geography of the domain.
- How the five linked resort sectors differ in base, terrain and role.
- Why the headline piste kilometre figure is not the same thing as a verified, fully connected route network.
- What the pass buys, and when a sector-specific pass is enough.
- What the altitude spread can and cannot tell us about snow reliability.
- Which claims require a live check before a trip.

## Narrative progression

1. **A large domain with a defined centre.** Introduce the Maurienne setting and the Ouillon-linked core; qualify the 310 km operator figure immediately.
2. **The network before the marketing.** Map the five linked sectors, then isolate Saint-Colomban as a disconnected or unresolved edge.
3. **Six names, different roles.** Compare each sector by base, height, connection and practical character; don't reduce them to a generic “family-friendly” label.
4. **Terrain and route implications.** Explain how a skier moves between sectors, once the route model is verified; separate lift rides from elapsed time.
5. **Snow is not one number.** Explain the contrast between high terrain and village elevations; use modelled history only when method, period and uncertainty are clear.
6. **Choose the pass for the skiing you will use.** Compare current products with age bands, dates and purchase channel explicit; keep unresolved Saint-Colomban validity visible.
7. **Choosing a base.** Explain what changes when staying at La Toussuire, Le Corbier, Saint-Sorlin, Saint-Jean or Les Bottières. Detail belongs on linked Level 4/5 pages.
8. **Who it suits, and the catches.** Make conditional judgements based on connected terrain, ability, elevation and trip style. State the limits plainly.
9. **Alternatives and next steps.** Compare like with like; link to the Portes du Soleil and Les 3 Vallées pages without repeating them.
10. **Method, sources and freshness.** Expose the scope of figures, the status of route modelling and the dates that must be checked again.

## Editorial controls

- No generic “hidden gem”, “something for everyone”, “quiet and uncrowded” or “excellent value” claims without evidence.
- No claim that six resorts are all linked unless current evidence supports it.
- Do not repeat 310 km in every section. Explain its definition once, then use it only where the scale matters.
- Separate the operator's figure from independent measurement and OSM-derived model output.
- Avoid making Saint-Colomban the whole story: it is a critical qualification, while the page must still explain the main connected network.
- No numerical snow-reliability claim until a reproducible model and station comparison exist.
- Atmosphere should come from the actual terrain, high plateau, Maurienne geography and settlement differences, not invented scenes or first-hand claims.
